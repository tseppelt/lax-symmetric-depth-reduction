We characterise the polynomials computed by bounded-depth matrix-symmetric algebraic circuits in terms of homomorphism polynomials. A circuit with variables $x_{i,j}$ for $i, j \in [n]$ is matrix-symmetric if every permutation of rows and columns extends to an automorphism of the circuit. The relevant complexity measures are the support size, the least $k$ such that every gate is fixed by the pointwise stabiliser of some set of at most $k$ rows and columns, and the support depth, the maximum number of wires along a path at which the minimal support of a gate is not contained in that of its parent. The patterns are measured by their $\Delta$-forestwidth $\mathrm{fw}_\Delta(F)$, the least width of a tree decomposition of $F$ of depth at most $\Delta$ with an empty root bag.

Over a field of characteristic zero, for $\Delta \geq 1$, $k \geq 2$, and $n \geq 2k+1$, a polynomial $p$ in the $n \times n$ matrix variables is computed by a reduced matrix-symmetric circuit of support size at most $k$ and support depth at most $\Delta$ if, and only if, $p$ is a linear combination of homomorphism polynomials $\hom_{F,n}$ of bipartite multigraphs $F$ with $\mathrm{fw}_\Delta(F) \leq k-1$. The two implications are stated separately: an explicit circuit construction for a single pattern, which additionally bounds orbit size, depth, and size, and an extraction of homomorphism polynomials from a circuit by induction on its gates. Along the way, we state that reduced matrix-symmetric circuits are rigid and that every matrix-symmetric circuit can be reduced without increasing size, depth, or orbit size.
